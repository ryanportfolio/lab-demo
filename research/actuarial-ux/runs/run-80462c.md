## 2. Executive synthesis

- **Strongest findings:** (1) Model-change management in incumbent workflows is dominated by manual file copies and naming conventions with weak audit trails — corroborated independently by a professional-body article and vendor-side surveys. (2) Massive pre-analysis labor is normalized: ~800 hours/quarter of spreadsheet prep before reserving analysis even starts at one large insurer (vendor case study, but concrete and detailed). (3) Model→production handoff is a re-coding step: actuarial logic is manually rebuilt by IT into rating engines, producing multi-version truth and ~150–192 day release cycles.
- **Most consequential latent problem:** practitioners have normalized "prep and reconcile" labor and file-copy versioning so thoroughly that it surfaces as job-description duties ("develop and improve existing control and versioning methodology") rather than complaints — a latent need invisible in complaint volume.
- **Strongest contradiction/negative evidence:** G2 reviews of Radar Live are largely positive on deployment ("fast and painless"), cutting against a blanket "incumbents are slow to deploy" story; deployment pain may concentrate in Excel-centric and legacy-engine shops, not all incumbent users.
- **Highest-value unanswered question:** how actuaries actually interact with model diagnostics day-to-day (charts vs exact values, what gets screenshotted into review decks) — no direct observational evidence reached this run.

## 6. Latent-problem analysis

1. **Prep-and-reconcile ritual (reserving).** Observable: 2 days/analyst/quarter of link-checking [S8] → normalized as "how the quarter starts" → unmet need: trustworthy automated roll-forward with reviewable diff → cost: compressed analysis/review window at exactly the highest-stakes time → alternative explanation: some prep is genuine judgment (data quality triage), not waste → validation: shadow a quarter close.
2. **Human-powered version control.** Observable: naming conventions, changelog tabs, job duties covering "versioning methodology" [S4][S16] → normalized as professional diligence → unmet need: automatic who/when/why capture with reviewable diffs → cost: irreproducibility, audit scramble, overwritten work → alternative explanation: git-style tooling exists but fails on binary/proprietary model formats — the gap may be format, not awareness → validation: ask teams that tried git and stopped.
3. **Simplify-to-meet-deadline.** Observable: documented runtime-driven simplifications [S9] → normalized as "materiality judgment" → unmet need: compute fast enough that fidelity isn't the calendar's hostage → cost: silent accuracy loss with no record of foregone fidelity → alternative explanation: some simplification is genuinely immaterial → validation: compare simplified vs full runs retrospectively.
4. **Triple-truth deployment.** Observable: Excel + filing + production versions [S13][S14] → normalized as "IT handoff" → unmet need: one governed artifact that is simultaneously reviewable, filable, executable → cost: translation defects, 5–6 month release latency [S6] → alternative explanation: G2 contradiction [S15] suggests modern rating platforms already solve this where adopted → validation: segment release-time data by tooling generation.
